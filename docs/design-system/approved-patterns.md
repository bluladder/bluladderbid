# Approved and rejected patterns

This registry starts with governance-level patterns. Add code-level examples only after Audit and Verification establish a successful implementation.

## Approved

### One dominant action

Use one visually dominant action for the current customer decision. Secondary actions remain available but quieter and clearly distinct.

### Outcome-led hero

Lead with a specific service outcome, local relevance, one principal conversion action, and credible supporting proof. Keep navigation and secondary offers from competing with the first action.

### Guided decision group

Present a small coherent set of options with concise differentiators, accessible selection state, and an obvious continuation action. Reveal secondary detail on demand.

### Truthful progress

Show real progress tied to meaningful stages. Do not use progress indicators that imply steps are complete before authoritative confirmation.

### Inline reassurance

Place short trust or privacy context next to the point of concern. Avoid stacking generic guarantees far from the decision.

### Recoverable form state

Preserve safe input, identify the exact field or system problem, and provide a clear retry or alternate path. Avoid clearing the form after failure.

### Real local proof

Use actual reviews, results, owner/team presence, vehicles, neighborhoods, and service examples with enough context to be credible.

### Mobile action continuity

Keep the primary action reachable after the customer reviews the necessary information. Sticky actions are acceptable when they do not obscure content, keyboard input, or browser controls.

## Rejected

### Card grid as default architecture

Do not turn every item or section into an equal card. Use sequence, typography, spacing, and editorial composition.

### Nested cards

Do not use a card inside a card for ordinary grouping. It weakens hierarchy and consumes mobile space.

### Gradient as hierarchy

Do not use multiple gradients to compensate for unclear content priority.

### Decorative urgency

Do not use bouncing buttons, artificial countdowns, fake scarcity, or urgent color without a truthful operational basis.

### Equal CTAs

Do not present several equally prominent actions with different destinations at the same decision point.

### Hidden error

Do not rely on toast-only form errors, color-only invalid states, or error copy that disappears before recovery.

### Logo repetition

Do not repeat the logo to manufacture brand presence. Use recognizable typography, color, photography, tone, and operational detail.

### Generic premium styling

Do not equate premium with black backgrounds, gold, ornamental serif type, excessive whitespace, or glossy effects.

### Styling-led business change

Do not change eligibility, pricing, scheduling, analytics, or booking behavior while presenting the work as visual polish.
